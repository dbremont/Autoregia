Substrate · Capability Self-Management

Agent Capability Self Management SystemACSMS

The agent's improvement engine — it manages the deliberate growth of the agent's own capabilities: detecting gaps, maturing them into committed improvement programs, and consolidating demonstrated capability back into the self-model and the catalog.

Formulation
An ACSMS is best understood by analogy to an athlete's training regime or an organization's R&D function. Performance today is produced by the current capability set; the regime exists to change that set deliberately — identifying weaknesses, scheduling practice, demanding evidence, and retiring what no longer serves.

It does not perform the agent's work (that is AWES) and does not choose what the agent values (that is AGS). It maintains the pipeline that converts intent into capability, so that growth is a managed flow rather than an accident of whatever work happens to teach something. It keeps improvement resource-feasible, demands evidence over exposure, and keeps the capability set current — consolidating what is held, reviewing what decays, and culling what no longer serves.

Why a Dedicated System

The capability ledger and the improvement flow are different functions:

GIS catalogs

What exists, where it lives, where the gaps are — a map, not a program.

PRAS proposes

Adaptations — including "the agent should become able to X". Reasoning, not development.

AIAS commits

Improvement intents enter the Active Intent Set — but know nothing of practice, evidence, or tier promotion.

ACSMS develops

Closes the loop as a first-class function: without it, gaps stay visible but unaddressed, and "knowing" drifts from "being able to".

Position in the Architecture

ACSMS is a substrate system: it sits beside AGS and GIS in the agent's foundation, and is fed by the loop's feedback arc. In VSM terms it is System 4 (Intelligence) directed inward — scanning the self for gaps and planning its development.
